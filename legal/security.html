Security

Security at Contoura Labs starts with a simple principle. We cannot lose, leak, or be forced to hand over data we never had. That is why almost every tool processes your files entirely in your browser.

The short version: When you upload a file to one of our tools, it never leaves your device. The processing happens locally, inside your browser, using JavaScript. We do not have a copy of your file. There is nothing on our servers to steal.

01 Our Security Philosophy

Most security programs focus on how to defend a perimeter around a large pile of user data. Our philosophy is different. We have chosen not to collect that data in the first place, which means there is no pile to defend and no perimeter to breach.

This does not mean we ignore security. It means we focus our security effort where it actually matters: on the integrity of the code we ship to your browser, on the safe handling of the small amount of information we do process (such as contact form submissions), and on transparency about how the system works.

We believe that security through obscurity is not security at all. The tools on this site use open source libraries that are publicly audited, and we describe our practices honestly in this document. If we ever change our approach, we will update this page rather than bury the change in legal fine print.

02 In Browser Processing

Almost every tool on Contoura Labs is designed so that your data never leaves your device. When you upload a PDF to be merged, an image to be compressed, or text to be reformatted, the file is read by JavaScript running inside your browser. The processing happens on your own computer, and the result is offered back to you as a download.

This is made possible by modern browser APIs such as the File API, Web Workers, Canvas, and WebAssembly. Together, these technologies allow us to perform complex operations on your data without ever sending it across the internet. You can verify this for yourself by opening your browser network tab while using any tool. You will see the page assets load, but you will not see your file being uploaded.

The few exceptions are tools that genuinely require an internet connection to function, such as the currency converter which fetches live exchange rates. Even in those cases, the data we send is a small request for current rates, not your personal information. Each tool that requires a network call clearly documents this on its own page.

03 No Data Storage

Because files are processed in your browser, we have no need to store them. We do not maintain a database of user files. We do not write uploaded files to disk on our servers. We do not retain temporary copies for any period of time. There is no backup tape, no log file, and no caching layer that contains the contents of anything you have processed.

The only information we do store is described in our Privacy Policy. In summary, we store the messages you send through the contact form, anonymous aggregated usage statistics, and standard server logs that contain IP addresses and request timestamps for up to 30 days. None of this contains the contents of files you have processed.

If a government agency, law enforcement body, or civil litigant were to serve us with a legal request for your tool data, the honest answer would be that we do not have it. We cannot provide what we never collected. This is not a policy commitment that we might reverse in the future. It is a structural property of how the Service is built.

04 Encryption in Transit

All traffic between your browser and our servers is encrypted using Transport Layer Security (TLS). This means that the HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and any other assets we serve cannot be read or modified by anyone intercepting your network connection, whether you are on a home network, a corporate network, or a public WiFi hotspot.

We enforce HTTPS using HTTP Strict Transport Security (HSTS), which instructs your browser to always connect to our website over an encrypted channel, even if you type the address without the https prefix. This protects against certain types of downgrade attacks.

When you submit our contact form, your message is also transmitted over HTTPS to our email delivery provider, who then forwards it to our team. We do not accept contact form submissions over unencrypted connections, and we do not send or receive email containing personal information over unencrypted channels.

05 Third Party and Supply Chain

Like every modern website, we rely on third party libraries and services. We take supply chain security seriously and apply the following practices.

  • Open source libraries are pulled from established package registries and pinned to specific versions to prevent unexpected changes.
  • Third party services are reviewed before integration and reassessed periodically. We prefer providers with published security policies and independent audits.
  • External scripts, such as fonts and the Tailwind CDN, are loaded from reputable providers over HTTPS.
  • We use Subresource Integrity (SRI) hashes on critical external scripts where supported, so that your browser will refuse to run a script that has been tampered with.
  • We monitor security advisories for the libraries we depend on and update them promptly when patches are released.

We accept that no supply chain is perfectly secure, and we design our system on the assumption that any single dependency could be compromised. This is one of the reasons we keep the data we collect to an absolute minimum.

06 Vulnerability Disclosure

We welcome and encourage responsible disclosure of security vulnerabilities. If you believe you have found a security issue in our website, our tools, or our infrastructure, please report it to us so we can investigate and address it.

You can reach our security team by emailing security@contouralabs.com. Please include a detailed description of the issue, the steps required to reproduce it, and any potential impact. If you would like credit for your discovery, let us know and we will acknowledge you in our disclosure notes once the issue is resolved.

We ask that you give us a reasonable amount of time to investigate and address the issue before publishing any details publicly, and that you do not access, modify, or delete data that does not belong to you. We will not take legal action against researchers who act in good faith and in accordance with these guidelines.

We aim to acknowledge receipt of vulnerability reports within 48 hours and to provide an initial assessment within 5 business days. Resolution timelines depend on the severity and complexity of the issue, and we will keep you informed of our progress.

07 Incident Response

Despite our best efforts, security incidents can never be ruled out entirely. We maintain an incident response plan that allows us to detect, contain, investigate, and recover from any incident that affects the security of the Service or the limited information we hold.

In the event of a confirmed incident affecting personal data, we will notify affected users without undue delay. Where required by law, we will also notify the relevant supervisory authority, typically within 72 hours of becoming aware of the incident. Our notification will describe the nature of the incident, the likely consequences, and the steps we are taking to address it.

Because the contents of files you process never reach our servers, an incident on our side cannot expose those files. The scope of any potential incident is therefore limited to the small amount of information described in our Privacy Policy, such as contact form submissions and server logs.

After any incident, we conduct a post mortem to identify root causes and to implement additional safeguards that prevent similar incidents in the future. A summary of significant incidents and the lessons learned will be published on this page.

08 Your Role in Security

Security is a shared responsibility. While we have designed the Service to protect your data by not collecting it in the first place, there are a few simple things you can do to keep yourself safe while using the Service.

  • Use a modern, up to date browser. Browser updates include important security patches. The Service supports the latest two versions of all major browsers.
  • Be cautious on public WiFi. Even though our traffic is encrypted, public networks can be hostile. Consider using a trusted VPN if you frequently work on public networks.
  • Verify the URL. Make sure you are visiting contouralabs.com and not a similarly named domain. Phishing sites often mimic legitimate services.
  • Treat contact form submissions carefully. Do not include sensitive personal information such as passwords, credit card numbers, or government identifiers in messages sent through the contact form.
  • Report suspicious behaviour. If something looks off, please let us know. A quick report from an attentive user is one of our best early warning systems.

If you have any questions about this Security page or our practices, please contact us at security@contouralabs.com or through our contact page. We are happy to answer questions from users, journalists, researchers, and prospective partners.
